Rubric 1:
Theme: Spatial Continuity and Layout Consistency
- Tip1: Evaluate whether the relative positions of characters, objects, and environmental landmarks are logically maintained throughout the video — across cuts when present, AND across camera moves within a single shot. Penalize teleporting subjects, props swapping sides without motivation, or background landmarks shifting impossibly.
- Tip2: Check that eyelines, screen directions, and physical interactions are spatially coherent. In multi-shot footage this means the 180-degree rule and consistent geography between shots; in single-shot footage this means a character's gaze and reach remain anchored to the same spatial referents as the camera moves.
- Tip3: Assess whether the spatial arrangement implied by close-ups is consistent with what wider framings reveal. If only a single framing is present, judge whether the geometry shown is internally plausible (no objects floating, no overlapping volumes, no impossible perspectives).
- Tip4: For single-shot videos that are clearly meant to depict a multi-location or multi-scene narrative, treat the absence of spatial transitions as a logic failure (the prompt's intended geography never gets established).

Rubric 2:
Theme: Action and Narrative Continuity
- Tip1: Assess whether physical movements flow seamlessly through the entire video. In multi-shot footage, watch the seam at every cut for momentum and pose preservation; in single-shot footage, watch for stuttering, dropped frames, sudden pose-resets, or actions that abruptly restart mid-motion.
- Tip2: Evaluate whether the sequence of events conveys a coherent narrative — clear cause-and-effect, logical action-reaction, motivated transitions. Penalize narratives that contradict themselves (e.g., a character "starts running" twice with no intervening rest).
- Tip3: Check the logical progression of prop usage and character states. A held object should not vanish and reappear, drawn weapons should remain drawn, doors that opened should stay open. This applies equally to single- and multi-shot footage.
- Tip4: For prompts that explicitly call for a sequence of distinct events, judge whether the video actually delivers that sequence. A prompt asking for "shot 0 → shot 1 → shot 2" rendered as a single static shot is a major narrative-continuity failure (label as low).

Rubric 3:
Theme: Scene and Environmental Consistency
- Tip1: Determine whether background elements, lighting, color palette, and overall environment remain stable and recognizable. In multi-shot footage check across cuts; in single-shot footage check across the duration as the camera moves or time passes.
- Tip2: Penalize the setting inexplicably changing, disappearing, or introducing contradictory elements (e.g., furniture appearing mid-shot, weather flipping from rainy to sunny without an indicated cut).
- Tip3: Verify that environmental details — weather, footprints, shadows, smoke — persist with internally consistent physics over time. A puddle that disappears between two adjacent moments fails this rubric regardless of whether a cut occurred.
- Tip4: For single-shot footage, treat sudden environment morphing (a wall that becomes a window mid-shot) as the most severe form of scene-inconsistency failure.

Rubric 4:
Theme: Character and Subject Consistency
- Tip1: Evaluate whether the identity, facial features, age, and overall physical appearance of every named subject are strictly maintained throughout the video. This applies equally across cuts and across long single shots.
- Tip2: Check for consistency in clothing, hairstyles, accessories, and design details (mecha armour panels, jewellery placement, embroidery patterns) — they must not morph or rearrange themselves over time.
- Tip3: Ensure props handled by characters remain consistent in appearance and possession. A sword in the right hand at second 1 must not appear in the left hand at second 4 unless the visual content shows the swap happening.
- Tip4: If multiple named characters appear, verify that they remain distinguishable from each other for the entire duration — same identity must not blend into another character's face.

Rubric 5:
Theme: Multi-Shot Rationality (Shot Choices vs. Narrative Need)
- Tip1: Judge whether the chosen shot structure is appropriate for the prompt. If the prompt describes a multi-event narrative requiring multiple framings (close-up + wide + reaction shot), penalize a single static shot that ignores all of them — the model failed to plan the cinematic structure.
- Tip2: When cuts are present, assess the rationality of shot scale choices (wide for context, close-up for emotion/detail) and how their juxtaposition advances the story. Cuts that feel arbitrary, identical-framing, or random should score lower.
- Tip3: Evaluate whether transitions are clean, intentional, and logical — penalize transitions buried in heavy blur, distortion, or chaotic morphing artefacts (a transformer-induced "stitch" rather than an editor's choice).
- Tip4: Judge the pacing and timing of cuts (or, equivalently for single-shot footage, the pacing of camera moves and beats within the shot). Cuts at illogical moments — mid-syllable, mid-blink, mid-impact — score low.
